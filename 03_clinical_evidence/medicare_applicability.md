# Medicare applicability and evidence limitations — 2026-11-24
Targeted web verification; not independent systematic review.
## Direct evidence
Carvalho 2016 randomized 97, 83 completed, 3 weeks. Composite pain improvement 1.5 vs 0.2 (difference 1.3 points); RMDQ improvement 2.9 vs 0.0. [1]
Kleine-Borgmann 2019 randomized 122 (63/59), 3 weeks. Pain changes -0.62 vs +0.11 (difference -0.73); subjective disability improved, objective mobility did not. [2]
Ikemoto 2020 randomized 52, 26 each. Mean ages 68.2 and 65.3 (SD 13.0 and 13.8) as extracted by review [4]. No significant added benefit for pain/function at 3 or 12 weeks. Active comparator included activity advice and psychological self-management education. [3,4] This is directly relevant to Medicare applicability; cannot dismiss as merely underpowered.
## Pooled analysis
CLBP review searched September 12 2024; four studies include injection modality, limiting transfer to oral protocol. Pain MD -0.62 (95% CI -1.09,-0.14); three-week subgroup -0.67 (-1.11,-0.23); later follow-up -0.27 (-0.91,0.36); disability normalized to 0–100 MD -5.01 (-9.03,-0.99). Very low certainty for pain. [4]
Broader MSK review seven trials 703 participants, low-certainty subjective improvements and moderate-certainty absence of objective functional benefit; all PROM studies high risk of bias. [5] Cohorts overlap; no summation.
## Coverage implications
Short initial course and explicit response assessment are reasonable proposals, not proof of benefit. Older cohort negative result materially weakens generalizability. Do not promise opioid savings or sustained improvement. Compare against active standard care, not no treatment alone. Consider prospective Medicare-age pragmatic study and separately test incremental RTM contribution. Require clinical review and full-text verification; no gate marked verified.

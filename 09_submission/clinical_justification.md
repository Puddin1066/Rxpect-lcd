# Clinical justification for requested coverage — draft
Adults with nonspecific chronic low-back pain are the proposed population. Request restricted adjunctive clinician-directed fully disclosed OLP care, with reassessment after a short initial course; not replacement of indicated treatment. A 2025 meta-analysis of four articles (171 OLP, 161 controls) estimated NRS pain difference -0.62 (95% CI -1.09 to -0.14; I² 31%). Certainty very low [1]. This supports a modest average short-term signal, not proof of clinically meaningful benefit for every patient, durable efficacy, opioid reduction or platform-specific benefit. The proposed response-dependent continuation is a risk-limiting policy hypothesis. Collect baseline pain and function, adherence and adverse events; stop or revise ineffective care. Establish responder thresholds with clinical reviewers.
CMS's acupuncture decision demonstrates consideration of patient-relevant pain/function, harms and Medicare generalizability; it adopted restricted coverage and stopping rules [2]. It does not establish OLP equivalence, reimbursement entitlement or a transferable statutory category.
Associated RTM is requested only if independently medically necessary, with device and coding eligibility verified. No direct evidence that RxPect adds benefit to OLP is established here. Clinical review, age-stratified evidence, full texts and regulatory assessment required before release.
